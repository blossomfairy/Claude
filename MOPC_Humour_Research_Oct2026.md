# MOPC Network: Audience Humour Research

Prepared October 16, 2026, for funny short-form video (Reels, TikTok, Facebook, YouTube Shorts).

## 1. What the research says

| Finding | Evidence strength | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Maritime humour runs on exaggeration, deadpan or dry delivery, local dialect, and small-town everyday detail | Moderate (comedy and folklore writing, not audience data) | Saltscapes, NBLE (Rhindress), Dalhousie archives |
| Maritimers distrust "big feeling," meaning showing off or pretension. Corny-but-witty jokes land better than slick ones | Moderate | Saltscapes (Bette MacDonald and Maynard Morrison profile) |
| Local jokes reflect the community's own concerns and values. Insider references beat generic jokes | Moderate (folklore study, West Pubnico) | Érudit / Rabaska |
| Nova Scotia accent and slang videos, Maritime puns, and dad jokes get steady engagement on TikTok | Weak to moderate (TikTok discover pages, no ranked data) | TikTok discover pages |
| Older adults enjoy humour more than younger people do, but complex jokes are harder to follow. Keep the joke simple | Moderate (academic review) | ResearchGate mini-review |
| Older adults dislike aggressive humour and are sensitive to jokes about old age | Moderate | ResearchGate mini-review |
| Older adults prefer self-enhancing humour, meaning laughing at life's frustrations to cope | Moderate (407-person study) | Springer, Current Psychology |
| Gen X (40 to 54) watch mostly on Facebook and YouTube, and 75% watch nostalgia content | Weak (industry blog) | Rev.com |
| Boomer women are the most likely Facebook sharers of cute animals and food photos | Weak (industry study) | ITWeb / Fractl |
| Telecom humour can sell. A WiFi-stealing-neighbour horror-comedy raised Globe Telecom's monthly sales 5x | Moderate (Google case study, Philippines) | Think with Google |
| A rural ISP had success flipping stereotypes about country life with tongue-in-cheek humour | Moderate (UK case) | MediaCat (Airband) |
| The buffering wheel is a universally recognised joke format, and ISPs already use it | Strong | Meme.com, Bountiful Fiber, UTOPIA Fiber |

**Gaps:** I found no data on Pictou County creators, no ad-testing study of humour for viewers aged 50+, and no funny campaign from a rural Canadian ISP. The best gap-filler is MOPC's own Meta and YouTube results: run 2 or 3 humour styles as A/B variants and compare them.

## 2. What makes this audience laugh

The sections below are my analysis, built on the findings above.

1. **Deadpan understatement.** The person on screen treats a ridiculous internet problem as perfectly normal.
2. **Shared rural suffering.** Examples: standing on the deck with the phone over your head, the one corner of the house with signal, "can you hear me now" family calls, the kid's game lagging at the worst moment. The point is "that's us," not "that's them."
3. **Local specificity.** Show real Pictou County details: winter, the drive to town, the Tim's parking lot WiFi, everyone knowing everyone. Get these from Amy or the client and don't invent them.
4. **Corny wordplay and dad jokes.** These work for Bob, Olivia, and Diane and are safe for the brand. Examples: "Fibre-ly reliable." "Wireless? More like worry-less."
5. **Laughing with the audience, never at them.** Bob is the clever one in his jokes, not the confused old man. Never mock age, rural life, or a competitor.

## 3. Brand guardrails for comedy

Each line has a fix that keeps the joke working:

- **No eye contact with the camera.** Talking-to-camera skits are out. Use POV text over the scene, mockumentary interviews where the person looks just off camera, or reaction shots.
- **No competitor comparisons.** Mock the situation, such as buffering or dropped calls, never another provider.
- **No promises.** The punchline can be relief, but the CTA must be "See what's available at your address."
- **No dashes and no exclamation marks** in captions. Every caption needs hashtags, a location tag, and a CTA.
- **No third-party brands or logos** on screen. Blur TV screens, game titles, and coffee cups.

## 4. Video concepts by persona

| # | Persona | Concept | Format | Hook (on-screen text) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Backyard Boomer Bob | Bob holds his tablet up in every room during a grandkids video call, ending on the deck in a snowstorm. Deadpan throughout | POV text, no dialogue | "POV: Sunday family call in rural Pictou County" |
| 2 | Bob | The "signal spot." Bob marks a chair by the window with tape, labelled "Internet Chair. Do not move." | 10-second gag | "Every rural house has one" |
| 3 | Old Town Road Olivia | Olivia's playlist cuts out mid-chorus while she's dancing in the kitchen. She freezes mid-move and waits | Freeze-frame comedy | "When the song buffers right at the good part" |
| 4 | Tech-Savvy Family | Mockumentary. Each family member "confesses" who's hogging the bandwidth. The dog is blamed last | Office-style interviews looking off camera | "Who's using all the internet. An investigation" |
| 5 | Tech-Savvy Family | A kid's game lags. Slow-motion "noooo" while Dad is on a work call in the next room | Slow-mo, sound-driven | "Peak hour in our house" |
| 6 | Professional Paul | Paul's frozen face on a video call. His coworkers wait politely. Cut to Paul "frozen" in real life too, holding the pose | Visual gag | "When you freeze on camera and decide to commit" |
| 7 | Student Sarah | Upload bar for an assignment at 11:58 pm with a dramatic horror-movie soundtrack | Horror parody (Globe-style) | "Assignment due at midnight. Upload at 99%" |
| 8 | Small Business Owner Sam | The card machine is "thinking." The customer and Sam make increasingly awkward small talk about the weather | Awkward-silence comedy | "The longest 30 seconds in retail" |
| 9 | All | Maritime slang explainer: "Rural internet words, translated." Examples: "Some slow" = buffering. "Wicked" = finally loaded | Text-on-screen countdown | "Rural internet, explained in Maritime" |
| 10 | All, local team | MOPC staff deadpan-read real (approved) customer questions, then answer helpfully. Local, warm, slightly dry | Staff skit, no eye contact | "Things Pictou County asks us" |

Every concept ends on the same beat: relief, then "Two ways to connect. See what's available at your address." Concept 9 can feature FibreFirst and FirstHome Wireless naturally.

## 5. How to test

- Run concepts 1, 4 and 9 first, since they cover the three biggest personas and three humour styles: deadpan, mockumentary and wordplay.
- Use Meta to compare 3-second hold rate, completion rate, and shares. Shares matter most for Bob and Olivia's Facebook audience.
- Keep the winning style and retire the weakest after 2 weeks.
